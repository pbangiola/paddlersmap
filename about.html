About Paddler’s Map

This map follows NOAA’s actual National Water Model river channels. The older regional courses and straight-line gauge connections have been retired.

River colors

Modeled flow uses NOAA’s latest discharge, 10th-percentile low, the provisional midpoint of its 25th and 75th percentiles, and a provisional two-year recurrence upper anchor. Colors interpolate continuously from brown through blue and yellow to red. Gray means numerical references are missing or not loaded.

Where two gauges have usable readings and references, observed colors take priority along a confirmed directed NWM path between them. Each gauge’s stage is normalized to its own low and official flood references; the two positions are averaged. Raw stages at different gauges are never averaged. Endpoint reaches are excluded because exact gauge positions along those reaches are unknown.

Connected gauges and launches

Gauge markers show only fresh, unit-compatible observations on verified connected sections. Gauges without usable data or a confirmed connection are hidden. Click gauges or rivers to inspect numbers, timestamps and sources. Missing observations restore modeled river colors. The connected-gauge and observed-color controls can be switched independently.

Launch markers preserve the existing curated coordinates and verification identities. Green marks a location in the verification registry; yellow marks an unverified location. National launch collection remains incomplete. Confirm current access locally.

Updates and coverage

Gauge readings refresh every three hours. The national major-river snapshot refreshes daily, while visible detail flows refresh on a fifteen-minute cache. Coverage of verified gauge connections is expanding and is not yet complete. Alaska, Hawaii and Puerto Rico/USVI lack the modeled anomaly references in NOAA’s service; verified observed sections can still be shown there where references and actual geometry are available.

Colors indicate relative water conditions, not paddling suitability or guaranteed navigability. A gauge measures height above its local reference, not canoe depth.

Sources: NOAA NWM and NWPS, USGS annual stage references, and curated launch records including OpenStreetMap contributors. Basemap: OpenFreeMap and OpenMapTiles, rendered with MapLibre. Code and documented methods.

Reading the map

Observed gauge-to-gauge colors take priority only on confirmed directed paths with current compatible stage references; elsewhere NOAA modeled flow colors apply. Both use actual NWM flowlines. The overview colors major rivers (stream order 6+); zoom in to load more colored reaches. Gray means missing, invalid, or not-yet-loaded flow references. NOAA does not publish the required anomaly anchors for Alaska, Hawaii, or Puerto Rico/USVI in this service; those domains use gray modeled geometry unless a verified observed section is available. Click a colored river to inspect its numbers.

Modeled relative flow conditions, not paddling suitability or guaranteed navigability. Modeled anchors: 10th-percentile low, provisional 25th/75th-percentile midpoint, and provisional 2-year recurrence upper. Observed colors use each gauge’s own low and flood stage references in feet.

Gauge markers have dark outlines and follow the flow scale. The launch inventory preserves curated coordinates and verification identities. Launches have white outlines: yellow is unverified; green appears in the existing verification registry.

Inferred gap colors

A gray reach can receive an estimated color only when NOAA’s published directed topology brackets it between two colored reaches of the same color source, with no known branching or confluence along the gap. The estimate interpolates normalized color positions by reach count, up to 50 missing reaches. It never creates discharge, stage or reference values. Click a river and expand technical details to see its endpoint IDs and estimated position. One-sided gaps and unknown or ambiguous paths stay gray. The topology cache is partial, so many gaps cannot yet be filled.

NOAA seven-day anomaly

The comparison toggle uses NOAA’s separate seven-day average streamflow percentile layer. Its legend reproduces the exact labels and PNG symbols published by NOAA, including its “Insufficient Data Available” class. The service does not supply a normal-category legend entry; the map does not invent one. This legend applies only to the NOAA comparison layer.

Reservoirs and connected lakes: Mississippi first

The reservoir/lake toggle shows identified NOAA NWM waterbody footprints along the Mississippi mainstem. NOAA does not supply a reservoir-versus-natural-lake classification in this layer, and its footprints may represent only part of a larger named lake. Existing NOAA names and IDs are preserved. The Lake Pepin button opens the first priority view.

A transparent surface tint is a visual estimate: the mean of normalized upstream and downstream channel reference positions. Missing main-channel reach colors interpolate by directed channel distance. Published NWPS routing supplies every link; actual line/polygon overlap identifies membership. The method follows the main channel, excluding minor tributaries as color anchors. At headwater order changes, the largest published upstream branches may supply the bracket. Existing measured colors and all discharge, stage and reference values remain unchanged.

Reservoir storage and dam operations can decouple inflow from outflow. The tint does not estimate reservoir discharge, water level, storage, current speed, release timing or navigability. Missing brackets, incomplete/cyclic routing, overlapping memberships and invalid polygons remain explicitly unavailable. Pool 2 currently has an invalid NOAA source polygon and is excluded. Click a footprint and expand technical details for the actual anchors, source values, timestamps and method.

Footprints and routing are cached separately from changing flow values. The daily modeled-flow job refreshes the small reservoir context snapshot; the browser checks it every fifteen minutes and uses current visible flow values when available. The Mississippi baseline is retained while GitHub Actions expands available coverage nationally across all stream orders. National files load by viewport; coverage is still incremental.

National expansion and line widths

A resumable Actions workflow expands waterbody coverage hourly and refreshes published numerical values daily. It queries all named and unnamed flows through authoritative footprints. The map loads regional files for the current view, retaining the Mississippi baseline. National snapshots older than 36 hours become unavailable as cached numerical anchors. Alaska currently lacks a NOAA NWM waterbody layer; Hawaii and Puerto Rico/USVI lack modeled anomaly anchors, so those source gaps remain explicit.

Our colored river lines now match NOAA’s published 1-point width: about 1.33 CSS pixels at 96 DPI, at every zoom. Density still increases when zooming in.
